# Methodology

## Purpose

This project seeks to organize information concerning the death of Corey Ruiz in a verifiable, transparent, and accessible manner.

It does not begin with an obligation to defend a predetermined account. Its purpose is to allow the public to examine sources, compare statements, and distinguish between documented facts, interpretations, and unresolved questions.

## Classification of information

### Documented fact

Information directly supported by one or more identifiable documents, videos, photographs, recordings, or records.

A documented fact should identify:

- the source;
- the date of the source;
- the corresponding link or reference;
- any relevant limitations.

### Attributed statement

A claim made by a person, institution, public agency, organization, or news outlet.

Recording a statement does not mean accepting it as true. The statement should clearly indicate who made it, when it was made, and its context.

### Interpretation

A conclusion or reading derived from the analysis of evidence.

Interpretations must remain separate from facts and explain what evidence supports them.

### Open question

A question that cannot responsibly be answered using the currently available information.

Open questions must not be presented as accusations or conclusions.

## Source hierarchy

Sources will generally be classified as follows:

1. Original files and primary evidence.
2. Official documents.
3. Direct witness statements.
4. Original recordings and publications.
5. Journalistic reports.
6. Secondary posts and social-media commentary.

A source's position on this list does not guarantee that it is correct. Every source must be evaluated individually.

## Conflicting accounts

When incompatible accounts exist:

- each account will be documented separately;
- its source will be identified;
- supporting or contradictory evidence will be noted;
- the contradiction will not be declared resolved without sufficient evidence.

## Translations

Documents and statements originally published in English may be translated into Spanish.

When exact wording is relevant:

- the original text will be preserved;
- the translation will be clearly identified;
- the translation will attempt to preserve meaning without adding interpretation.

## Corrections

Errors will be corrected publicly.

Every significant correction should indicate:

- what information was changed;
- why it was changed;
- when the change was made;
- what source justified the correction.

Git history will allow prior versions of files to be reviewed.

## Privacy and safety

Sensitive personal information belonging to witnesses, family members, or other individuals will not be published unless it is already legitimately part of the public record and necessary to understand the case.

Private files, unpublished original evidence, and contact information will remain outside the public repository.
